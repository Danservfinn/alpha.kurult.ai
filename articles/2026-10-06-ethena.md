## Research view

USDe remains the largest product of its kind and the mechanism has survived every stress event thrown at it. The problem is the income statement: sUSDe yield tracks perp funding, funding compresses as the basis trade gets crowded, and every basis point of compression cuts the number that markets use to value ENA. The +37% month is beta on an AI-and-alt risk bounce, not new information about yield. We underweight against the sector and re-underwrite if UStb institutional demand materially replaces the funding-dependent carry.

## First principles: the basis trade at scale

Ethena's primitive is an arbitrage identity, and identities do not have bull markets, they have spreads. USDe is minted by shorting perps against a long spot basis. Hold BTC and ETH spot, short the equivalent perps, collect the funding rate that leveraged longs pay. The dollar peg is collateralized by that spread position. Nothing about this is exotic: it is the cash-and-carry trade that has existed on every futures market since the 19th century, packaged as a stablecoin.

The physics of the yield: sUSDe passes funding through to holders. Funding is positive when leverage demand is high, negative when it is not. The yield is therefore cyclical by construction, and it compresses as more capital chases the same basis, which is exactly what Ethena's own growth does. Every new USDe minted is a new participant bidding away the spread that funds the product. The machine is built to erode its own fuel.

The valuation link: ENA was priced at peak funding, and funding has compressed. The +37% month is beta, not spread. The first-principles questions are mechanical. What is the average funding rate over the last 90 days versus the 365 before it? What share of USDe growth is now driven by UStb, the tokenized-T-bill variant that does not depend on funding at all? If institutional UStb demand replaces the carry trade as the growth engine, ENA becomes a different asset with a different, steadier cash-flow story. Until that mix shift shows in the data, this is a cyclical yield instrument priced as if the cycle were permanent.

## Overview
Ethena issues USDe, a synthetic dollar minted against short perpetual-futures positions in BTC and ETH, with staked yield (sUSDe) funded by perpetual funding rates and basis spreads. After a severe contraction from late 2025 into mid-2026, the project spent 2026 reinventing itself around revenue buybacks, tokenized-equity collateral, institutional distribution, and a first-of-its-kind bank research rating.

## Fundamentals and tokenomics
- ENA trades near $0.249, market cap about $3.3B (rank ~39). Circulating supply is ~13.4B of a hard 15B cap, so emission is nearly complete. A roughly 140-171M ENA tranche unlocked on October 5, 2026 (press estimates varied; some outlets erroneously reported 1.4B).
- ATH is $1.52 (April 11, 2024); the price sits ~84% below it. The all-time low (~$0.070) was set in 2026 during the deleveraging.

- sUSDe APY is ~5.4% (up from ~3.5% in April 2026); staked sUSDe TVL is ~$1.2B. Protocol TVL is ~$5.4B versus ~$15.0B a year ago.

## Bull case
Buybacks convert real revenue into persistent bid; USDe supply is inflecting up again; institutional rails (Coinbase, FalconX, Nasdaq-listed treasury vehicle) plus equity-perp collateral massively enlarge the addressable basis trade; sell-side validation from Standard Chartered.

## Bear case and risks
Funding-rate dependence: APY fell to ~3.5% in April amid poor positioning, and extended negative funding would impair the model. Redemption reflexivity: the 2025-26 contraction showed USDe shrinks fast in risk-off. Equity collateral adds TradFi venue and counterparty risk (Binance), while the NY Fed's Liberty Street blog (Jun 24) flagged synthetic stablecoins as a stability concern and GENIUS Act treatment of yield-bearing synthetic dollars remains unresolved. Custody concentration and residual unlocks overhang the token.

## Outlook (12 months)
Base case: ENA consolidates in a $0.15-$0.45 band while buybacks run and USDe rebuilds toward $6-8B; positive funding plus equity-basis expansion supports re-rating toward the lower end of bank targets. Downside case re-emerges if funding inverts or regulation constrains the synthetic-dollar model. High-beta, polarized setup: size accordingly.
