Tesla sells two things that show up in the accounts: a finished electric vehicle, and a kilowatt-hour of storage. The car still pays the bills. Storage is the second engine. The accounts do not contain a paid driverless mile. The market is treating that mile as if it were already the product.

The number to remember is $6,645. That is automotive sales gross profit per cash delivery in the second quarter of 2026. Price $42,337, cost $35,692, profit $6,645, before operating expenses and before the $12,057 of company capex that landed in the same quarter for every vehicle delivered. Derived from the Q2 2026 update exhibit. Cash deliveries are total deliveries minus deliveries subject to operating lease accounting, which is the company's own denominator for average cost per vehicle.

## What the accounts show

The car and the pack are a real hardware business. Cost per cash delivery did not fall across the five quarters in the Q2 update. Regulatory credits fell. Capex in the latest quarter was larger than sales gross profit per car. Trailing operating income is $4.4 billion. The accounts do not contain a paid mile.

## Drivers

Four drivers carry the view.

1. The unit. Gross profit per cash car is about $6,645. Volume is not the fragile input. A $2,000 cost miss on the Q2 cash-delivery count wipes about $0.95 billion of sales gross profit at constant volume. Cost and price move the unit. Volume does not, inside the range we checked.

2. The mile is not a line. Paid autonomous miles are not in the 10-Q revenue table. The Q2 update draws cumulative paid robotaxi miles on a chart and does not print the number in the HTML text. Company statements outside that filing text are operational counts, not financial figures. Business Insider, reporting the Q2 call, says Tesla put cumulative paid miles at nearly 2.5 million by the end of Q2, and that figure includes trips with a safety monitor. Electrek, Sep 3, 2026, reports Tesla's announcement of 1 million unsupervised miles. Cite both as company statements, not as a filing line, and not as an annual rate.

3. The accounts do not contain the mile. Trailing operating income is $4.37 billion. Robotaxi and Optimus are company timing claims. They are not revenue lines. The Oct 2 delivery print was already public. The Oct 21 financials are not out.

4. The quarter spent the gross profit. Q2 free cash flow was negative $1,092 million. Trailing free cash flow was still positive. The binding quarter was not the year. It is still the freshest cash print.

## What the Q3 10-Q would have to show

Oct 21, 2026 is the scheduled print. This is the measurement, not an instruction. Each line below is what would have to be on the page to move the research view. A delivery count alone will not do it. The Oct 2 exhibit already printed 486,532 deliveries.

- Cash deliveries. The Oct 2 exhibit prints 486,532 deliveries and "1%" subject to operating lease accounting. It does not print the cash-delivery count. One percent of 486,532 is not an integer, so the exact count is unknown. To move the view, the 10-Q or the update would have to print cash deliveries and an automotive sales gross profit per cash car that breaks the $6,645 unit. A higher delivery count with the same unit does not move the view.
- SpaceX mark, on the Other income line. Q2's unrealized gain was $1,005 million, about 90% of net income to common, leaving about $109 million without it. A Q3 Other income line that again exceeds Q2 operating income of $398 million would mean the bottom line is still the mark. A line near zero, with operating margin still near 1.4% and no mile disclosure, removes that cushion and leaves the cautious view in place. This note does not use a SpaceX share close. The mark is Level 2, including the marketability discount. The sign and size of the Q3 gain are unknown.
- Paid miles and unsupervised miles. Company statements in hand are nearly 2.5 million cumulative paid miles at the end of Q2, including safety-monitor trips, and 1 million unsupervised miles announced Sep 3. Those are operational counts, not financial figures. To move the view, the filing would have to print a paid-mile figure with a denominator.
- Cybercab fleet size. Not in the filings saved here. One secondary source, Not a Tesla App, read 20:03 ET Oct 4, says 169 Cybercabs were registered in Austin. That is a registration count from a secondary page, not a Tesla disclosure, and not cars confirmed in service. To move the view, the 10-Q would have to print a fleet size.
- Cover count. The July 16 cover count and the Q2 basic share count differ by about 712 million. The inference that this is the unearned award block is not a filing table. A Q3 cover count still near 3.95 billion makes that branch more likely. A jump of hundreds of millions makes dilution additive. That is the bar for this line.
- R&D and SBC. Q2 R&D was $2,371 million, against $1,589 million a year earlier. Quarterly SBC was $1,151 million. Both sit inside the 1.4% operating margin. To move the view, these lines would have to fall enough to lift operating margin without a one-time mark. Confirming them near the Q2 levels leaves the view where it is.

## What would change the view

- Oct 21, 2026. Automotive gross margin excluding credits above 16%, and energy margin back above 25%: the base holds. Credits under $100 million and energy margin under 18%: the bear path.
- A paid-mile number with a denominator, in the update or the 10-Q: the bull path opens. A chart without a number does not count. The Q2 chart did not count.
- A draw on the facilities in 2026: contradicts the Sept 29 sentence. Treat it as a cash signpost.
- Optimus units sold to a third party, disclosed: an option, not a reason to rerate the car.
- Share count on the Q3 10-Q cover. If it is still about 3.95 billion, the award-shares-already-included branch is more likely. If it jumps by hundreds of millions, dilution is additive.

A kill of the cautious view is a filing that shows robotaxi gross profit large enough to matter next to the $3.1 billion quarterly car gross profit. A kill of a long view, were one on the table, would be credits near zero, energy margin still near 20%, and capex still above operating cash flow, with no mile disclosure. No position is taken in this note.
